CSE 227
Graduate Computer Security
Winter 2026  ·  Tues & Thurs, 11:00am  ·  WLH 2204
Graduate

This course focuses on computer security, exploring a range of topics — from systems security, to web security, edge security, and privacy — to illustrate modern research challenges and standards for advancement. It is not designed as a tutorial course, but rather to give students the context to understand current security research and evaluate their interest in the field. The course examines both the defensive and offensive sides of the field. At the conclusion of the course, students will have the foundation to conduct research in computer security and apply the latest research to a particular area of practice.

Project Ideas

You are encouraged to come up with your own idea. Some starter questions from a usable privacy and security perspective:

  • Literature review (SOK papers accepted — must use NLP/mixed methods technique)
  • Privacy pop-ups and the end user response
  • Security analysis of free tools for tracking flights
  • How have AI privacy notices and terms changed over the last few years?
  • How are GenAI video creators negotiating privacy?
  • How are everyday social media users negotiating privacy re: GenAI and personal image?
  • How is open source intelligence used on TikTok? What educational initiatives might support those being found?
  • How has GenAI impacted cybercrime? What solutions have prevailed?
  • What can cybersecurity professionals learn from physical security professionals?

CSE 191/290
AI News Roundup
Winter 2026  ·  Wednesdays, 9:00–10:20am  ·  EBU3B 4140
Seminar

A discussion- and presentation-based AI news roundup. Each week, participants take turns presenting and leading discussion on AI-related news, research, tools, or developments they find timely, interesting, and worth examining. The scope of AI is intentionally broad, allowing participants to bring in perspectives from their own disciplines and areas of expertise.

The goal is to help participants become more informed about how AI is shaping both the world and academic research. The class is designed as a supportive space to learn, ask questions, and explore ideas together. Presentations may take the form of news summaries, research discussions, demos, or hands-on workshops.
